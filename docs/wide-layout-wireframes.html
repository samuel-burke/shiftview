Design proposal · iPad & desktop

ShiftView wide layouts

ShiftView works well on a phone. On an iPad it shows the phone layout with empty margins on both sides, and on a monitor it spreads a few cards across a wide, mostly empty canvas. This page shows where the layout breaks, proposes a four-size system, and wireframes each screen at iPad portrait, iPad landscape, and desktop sizes.

Diagnosis

One breakpoint at 900px decides everything

Every page switches layout at one hard-coded width, [@media(min-width:900px)] (and useIsDesktop(900) in JavaScript). Below 900px you get the phone layout, capped at 480px. At 900px and above you get a 220px sidebar and a two-column grid. Every iPad in portrait is under 900px. Every iPad in landscape and every monitor is above it.

High

iPad portrait gets a phone in a box

Every page's <main> is max-w-[480px] mx-auto, and the bottom nav is pinned at 480px. On an 820px iPad Air, 340px (41% of the width) is empty, and touch targets sit in the middle of the screen, away from your thumbs.

app/pageClient.tsx:992 · components/BottomNav.tsx:27 · same pattern in schedule, clock, draft, reports, admin, coverage

Medium

iPad landscape is a squeezed desktop

At 1180px the 220px sidebar takes 19% of the width. The dashboard's 1fr_380px grid leaves the coverage chart about 500px wide, while the right column holds a long scrolling list.

components/SideNav.tsx:22 (w-[220px]) · pageClient.tsx:995 · schedulePageClient.tsx:1255 (1fr_320px) · draftPageClient.tsx:412 (1fr_400px)

Medium

Big monitors: either stretched or stranded

The dashboard has no max width, so the coverage timeline (fixed at 150px tall) becomes a 1,240 × 150 strip at 1920px. Other pages cap themselves at different widths: Admin and Coverage at 672px, Clock at 600px, Reports at 896px. They float in empty space and don't line up with each other.

CoverageTimeline.tsx:269 (height 150) · adminPageClient.tsx:162 · clockPageClient.tsx:599 · reportsPageClient.tsx:688

Medium

Overlays cover what you're comparing against

On desktop, employee detail, requests, open shifts and draft shift editing open as right drawers over the page, so you can't see coverage while you approve or edit. Availability and store hours still open as 480px bottom sheets on a 27-inch screen. Settings is a modal capped at 90% of the viewport height.

EmployeeDrawer.tsx:286 · RequestsDrawer.tsx:140 · AvailabilitySection.tsx:310 · StoreHoursSection.tsx:184 · settingsPageClient.tsx:778

Medium

No view built for a manager at a desk

Managers build schedules on a laptop, but there's no team-wide week grid with people as rows and days as columns. The Planner's "Schedule At a Glance" is seven day chips in a 400px column, and approvals live only in a drawer.

draftPageClient.tsx:463–480 · RequestsDrawer.tsx

Low

Desktop input isn't used

There are no keyboard shortcuts beyond Escape, few hover affordances, and no right-click or multi-select. The store-wide iPad, a common fixture in retail back rooms, has no shared clock-in (kiosk) mode.

keydown handlers only in EmployeeDrawer, MessageThread, UserMenu

Proposal

Four size classes instead of one cliff

Base layouts on window width, not device type. iPad Split View and Stage Manager produce every width from 320px to 1366px, so an iPad can land in any of the four classes.

/* app/globals.css: Tailwind v4 breakpoints */
@theme {
  --breakpoint-md: 600px;   /* medium: iPad portrait, split view */
  --breakpoint-lg: 1024px;  /* expanded: iPad landscape, laptop */
  --breakpoint-xl: 1440px;  /* wide: desktop monitor */
}

// hooks/useBreakpoint.ts replaces useIsDesktop
type Size = "compact" | "medium" | "expanded" | "wide";
export function useBreakpoint(): Size

// before: [@media(min-width:900px)]:grid-cols-[1fr_380px]
// after:  md:grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-[1fr_360px] xl:grid-cols-12
  • compactUnder 600px. Today's phone layout, unchanged. Bottom tabs, bottom sheets, single column.
  • medium600 to 1023px. Remove the 480px cap. Add a 72px icon rail. Two-column content. Sheets become centered dialogs. Touch targets stay at 44px or larger.
  • expanded1024 to 1439px. The rail can be pinned open to 220px. List and detail sit side by side, and detail opens in a pane instead of a drawer.
  • wide1440px and up. Persistent 240px sidebar, 12-column grid, content capped at 1680px. Shows denser manager views such as the week grid, the approvals inbox, and the inspector pane.
Navigation

Bottom tabs → rail → sidebar

Today, iPad users get three bottom tabs and must open the user menu to reach Planner, Admin, Reports or Settings. A rail shows every destination at medium size while using only 72px. The clock-status dot moves into the rail so it's visible on every screen, just as ClockStatusBadge is in the current sidebar.

Sheets & panes

One rule for every overlay

At compact size, use bottom sheets as today. At medium size, use a centered dialog of 560px at most. At expanded and wide sizes, open detail in a side pane that pushes content over instead of covering it, so the list or chart behind stays visible and usable. Short pickers become popovers anchored to the control that opened them.

Principles

Rules to hold every screen to

Cap widths by page type

Use three widths, applied the same way everywhere. Forms and settings: 720px. Reading and lists: 960px. Dashboards and grids: 1680px. Use 24px gutters at medium and expanded sizes and 32px at wide. Don't let a page decide its own number.

Charts keep their shape

Give the coverage timeline an aspect ratio between 2.5:1 and 3.5:1 with a minimum height, instead of a fixed 150px. When there's spare width, add another panel next to it rather than stretching the chart.

Fill wide screens with information

Use extra width for more context, such as coverage next to approvals, the week next to the day, or a list next to its detail. Bigger cards don't help. Every wide layout should save a tap compared with the phone.

iPads are touch first

Keep 44px targets, swipe-to-dismiss and haptics at medium and expanded sizes. Only add hover-only features (tooltips, row actions) under @media (hover: hover), so an iPad with a keyboard and trackpad gets them and a bare iPad doesn't lose anything.

Keyboard on desktop

← → change the week, T jumps to today, N creates a new shift, A / D approve or deny the selected request, / searches people, Esc closes the pane. List them in a ? overlay.

One DOM, rearranged by CSS

Continue the pattern already in schedulePageClient.tsx: one React tree, reordered with grid placement. Keep the JS hook for behavior only, such as drag versus no drag, and never for layout. This avoids the layout shift that AppShell.tsx's comment warns about.

Same density in both themes

Every wireframe here works in the light theme too. Shift-type colors (opener amber, mid blue, closer purple) carry the week grid, so check them on white at the small block sizes used there.

Safe areas and split view

The rail and sidebar take env(safe-area-inset-left) in landscape. Test at 1/3, 1/2 and 2/3 Split View on a 12.9-inch iPad (about 420, 678 and 980px) so each width lands in its intended size class.

Rollout

Order of work

Each phase can ship on its own and improves iPads and desktops immediately. Phase 1 gives the biggest improvement for the least work.

  1. Size classes, rail, and remove the 480px cap S–M

    Add the @theme breakpoints and useBreakpoint(). Replace [@media(min-width:900px)] with lg: across pages, which is mechanical. Add a NavRail for medium size and hide BottomNav at md:. Change page <main> from max-w-[480px] to md:max-w-none and give each page a medium two-column arrangement.

    components/AppShell.tsx · SideNav.tsx · BottomNav.tsx · TopBar.tsx · hooks/useIsDesktop.ts · app/*/…PageClient.tsx · app/globals.css

  2. Panes instead of drawers M

    Add a shared DetailPane slot in AppShell that pushes content at expanded and wide sizes. Move EmployeeDrawer, TimeCardDrawer and DraftShiftSheet onto it, and move Availability and Store Hours into inline editors. Only the container changes. Drawer contents stay as they are.

    components/AppShell.tsx · EmployeeDrawer.tsx · TimeCardDrawer.tsx · DraftShiftSheet.tsx · AvailabilitySection.tsx · StoreHoursSection.tsx

  3. Manager week grid L

    Add a new view with people as rows and days as columns, with an open-shifts row and a coverage footer. Drafts and published shifts appear in the same grid. Click a block to open the inspector, and use drag-to-move behind a pointer check. This is the main reason managers will use a laptop.

    new: components/WeekGrid.tsx · app/draft/draftPageClient.tsx · app/api/schedules, /api/drafts (existing)

  4. Requests inbox and Settings layout M

    Turn RequestsDrawer into a /requests route with filters, a list and detail, plus a coverage-impact preview. Turn the Settings modal into a section nav with a form page.

    components/RequestsDrawer.tsx → app/requests/ · app/settings/settingsPageClient.tsx

  5. Shared iPad kiosk clock M–L

    Add a store-wide clock-in mode for a fixed iPad: a roster of who's scheduled now, a PIN keypad, and an auto-lock timer. It needs a kiosk session type and a PIN per employee, so it requires a product decision before any design work.

    app/clock/clockPageClient.tsx · app/api/punches · new migration for kiosk PINs
